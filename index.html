    About Hike Planner 3D

    Draw a route on the map or import a GPX file, and the app builds a 3D model of the hike: terrain, walking times, overnight stops, huts and water on the way, and the weather for your days. Saved hikes open without a connection.

    Where the information comes from

    BRouter, using OpenStreetMap paths and their difficulty grades.

    World Imagery, Powered by Esri. Sources: Esri, Vantor, GeoEye, Earthstar Geographics, CNES/Airbus DS, USDA, USGS, AeroGRID, IGN, and the GIS User Community. Shown only while you have a connection, and never saved on the phone.
    Map
    OpenTopoMap (CC-BY-SA), from OpenStreetMap data and SRTM heights.
    Terrain heights
    AWS Terrain Tiles (Mapzen), about 10 to 30 m resolution in the mountains. The light and the shade in hollows are worked out from them on your phone.
    Huts, passes, water, place names
    OpenStreetMap, through Overpass and Nominatim.
    Trail check
    OpenStreetMap paths along the route, through Overpass: their grades, how visible they are, the marked routes and their waymarks, junctions and signposts, cliffs, glaciers, stream crossings, ladders and chains. In Switzerland also the closures and diversions of the Swiss hiking trails (Federal Roads Office, through geo.admin.ch).
    Live guide map
    The hike's own map picture, which works offline; with a connection, the Swiss national map © swisstopo in Switzerland and OpenTopoMap elsewhere.
    Emergency numbers
    112 everywhere in Europe, on any network that reaches your phone; Rega 1414 in Switzerland (+41 333 333 333 from a phone that is not Swiss), mountain rescue 140 in Austria, Salvamont +40 725 826 668 in Romania. Checked in November 2026.
    Weather
    Open-Meteo: the forecast from national weather models, with the wind near the ground and higher up, and climate statistics from the ERA5 reanalysis.

    Good to know

    Likely weather. Up to 15 days ahead, the chance of each weather type is the share of the 51 runs of the ECMWF forecast model that give it. Further ahead, it is how often each type happened at this place on these dates over the last 10 years (ERA5 weather records). Valley fog has no percentage, because both sources are too coarse to tell a foggy valley from the sunny ridge above it.

    Times use the DIN 33466 hiking formula (4 km/h on the flat, 300 m/h up, 500 m/h down; the larger plus half the smaller), scaled by your pace, with 10 minutes' rest an hour if you choose rests. Bad weather adds rough allowances: 4–20% on wet ground, 25–70% on snow, more on ice, about 10% in thick cloud or strong gusts.

    Effort is km-effort, as in trail running: each kilometre and each 100 m of climbing count 1. Under 15 is easy, 15–25 moderate, 25–35 hard, over 35 very hard. Daylight is the time left between your arrival and sunset on your tightest day: amber under an hour, red after dark.

    Weather types are typical patterns, timed against your walk, using temperature statistics for this place and height. The forecast uses Open-Meteo's hourly forecast at points along the route; it appears 16 days before your start and updates when you open the hike with a connection.

    Wind starts from the forecast near the ground and higher up (about 1,500 and 3,000 m); for a weather type, it blows from where it did on such days in the ERA5 records for these dates. Your phone then lets it flow over the terrain: faster over crests and passes, sheltered behind them, turned along valleys, and with light breezes up the slopes by day and down them at night. It is a simple model, not a full weather simulation, so expect real gusts to vary more.

    Best views are found from the terrain alone: how far you can see, how high the mountains around you stand, how deep the valleys drop and which named summits are in clear sight. Clouds, forest and buildings are not counted. First person walks the route at true height, with the light and weather of the hour you would be there.

    Inventory keeps your own gear on this phone only. The packing advice names the first item of each kind, and uses general words where you have none.

    Safety is checked against the paths mapped in OpenStreetMap. Grades follow the SAC scale: T1 hiking, T2 mountain hiking, T3 demanding (hands at times), T4 and up alpine. In Switzerland the waymarks tell the grade too: yellow T1, white-red-white T2–T3, white-blue-white T4 and up. Steep drops beside the path come from the terrain heights: ground that keeps falling at 42° or more from 10 to 55 m out. A stretch is never called too hard for a beginner from the terrain alone, only from the map's grades, via ferratas, glaciers, closures or long stretches with no path. The check runs once with a connection and stays with the hike; it is done again after two months.

    Find an easier way asks the BRouter trail router for a way round each hard place the safety check found: with no stretches harder than T2 (T3 too, for an alpine stretch, a via ferrata, a glacier or a closure), starting 1.5 km and then 5 km before and after it. A way round is offered when its detour stays reasonable, and it is saved as a new hike next to this one, so you can compare them.

    Live guide uses your phone's GPS and works without a connection. It warns you with a sound when you are more than 40 m from the route for three positions in a row (more when the signal is weak, and never on a very weak signal), points the way back, and chimes as you come to each junction. It keeps the screen on, which uses more battery: carry a power bank. Keep the guide open on the screen: with the phone locked, a web app cannot follow you or warn you. On an iPhone the sound follows the ring/silent switch, and web apps cannot vibrate. Your position stays on your phone unless you share it.

    Difficulty grades are OpenStreetMap's sac_scale tags; steepness is the slope of the path in degrees, averaged over about 200 m. Water points are springs and taps marked on OpenStreetMap; check that they run.

    Heights come from satellite data, so summits can read a little low. The plan is a guide: on the ground, follow the waymarks, check the national weather service, and turn back when conditions change.
