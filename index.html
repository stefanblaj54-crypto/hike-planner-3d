Building your plan

This takes from a few seconds to about a minute, depending on the length of the route and the connection. Keep the app open.

    About Hike Planner 3D

    Draw a route on the map or import a GPX file, and the app builds a 3D model of the hike: terrain, walking times, overnight stops, huts and water on the way, and the weather for your days. Saved hikes open without a connection.

    Where the information comes from

    Trails and routing
    BRouter, using OpenStreetMap paths and their difficulty grades.
    Satellite pictures
    Sentinel-2 cloudless - https://s2maps.eu by EOX IT Services GmbH (Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2025). Free for personal use.
    Close-up aerial pictures in and around Switzerland
    SWISSIMAGE © swisstopo, free geodata of the Swiss Confederation.
    Map
    OpenTopoMap (CC-BY-SA), from OpenStreetMap data and SRTM heights.
    Terrain heights
    AWS Terrain Tiles (Mapzen), about 10 to 30 m resolution in the mountains. The light and the shade in hollows are worked out from them on your phone.
    Huts, passes, water, place names
    OpenStreetMap, through Overpass and Nominatim.
    Weather
    Open-Meteo: the forecast from national weather models, and climate statistics from the ERA5 reanalysis.

    Good to know

    Likely weather. Up to 15 days ahead, the chance of each weather type is the share of the 51 runs of the ECMWF forecast model that give it. Further ahead, it is how often each type happened at this place on these dates over the last 10 years (ERA5 weather records). Valley fog has no percentage, because both sources are too coarse to tell a foggy valley from the sunny ridge above it.

    Times follow the DIN 33466 hiking formula, adjusted for your pace, rests and the weather. Heights come from satellite data, so summits can read a little low. The plan is a guide: on the ground, follow the waymarks, check the national weather service, and turn back when conditions change.

      Day by day

      Arrival times use the start time, pace, rests and the weather above.

      DayRouteDistanceClimbDescentHighestWalkingArriveLikely weather

      A plan, not a guarantee. The line follows OpenStreetMap paths (or your file) and heights come from satellite terrain data, so summits and passes can read a little low. On the ground, follow the waymarks and signposts, and check the local rules for camping and huts.

      Times use the DIN 33466 hiking formula (4 km/h on the flat, 300 m/h up, 500 m/h down; the larger plus half the smaller), scaled by your pace. Bad weather adds rough allowances: 4–20% on wet ground, 25–70% on snow, more on ice, about 10% in thick cloud or strong gusts.

      Weather types are typical patterns, timed against your walk, using temperature statistics for this place and height. Forecast uses Open-Meteo's hourly forecast at points along the route; it appears 16 days before your start and updates when you open the hike with a connection. Before you go, also check the national weather service and its warnings.

      Difficulty grades are OpenStreetMap's sac_scale tags (for routes drawn here); imported files show steepness instead: the slope of the path in degrees, averaged over about 200 m. Water points are springs and taps marked on OpenStreetMap; check that they run.
