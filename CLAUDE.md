# Hike Planner 3D: working notes

A hiking planner PWA in plain JavaScript: no build step, no framework, no backend. It is published by GitHub Pages from `main` (root) at https://stefanblaj54-crypto.github.io/hike-planner-3d/ and installed on the owner's phone from that address. The owner is not a developer: he asks for changes in plain language, and pushing to `main` is how a change goes live.

## How a change goes live

- Push to `main`. GitHub Pages republishes in about a minute. `.nojekyll` skips Jekyll, so files are served as they are.
- The installed app checks for changes each time it opens or comes back to the screen (`checkUpdate` in js/app.js asks `check()` in sw.js). The service worker downloads the app's own files again, which is everything it keeps except `lib/`, `fonts/` and `icons/`. If any file changed, it swaps them all in at once and tells the page. The page reloads straight away on the hikes list or About; anywhere else it shows a toast and reloads when the person goes back to the list.
- An ordinary change needs no version bump in sw.js. Do bump `VERSION` in js/app.js for each release: it shows in About and in the menu.
- Bump `VERSION` in sw.js only when a file is added, removed or renamed, or when anything in `lib/`, `fonts/` or `icons/` changes, because those are kept as fixed and only a new service worker refreshes them. Regenerate the offline file list at the same time, from the repository root:

```sh
python3 - <<'EOF'
import os, re, json
fs = ['./', 'index.html', 'app.css'] + sorted(os.path.join(r, f) for d in ['fonts', 'icons', 'img', 'js', 'lib'] for r, _, n in os.walk(d) for f in n if not f.endswith('.txt')) + ['manifest.webmanifest']
s = open('sw.js').read()
s = re.sub(r'const SHELL=\[.*?\];', lambda m: 'const SHELL=' + json.dumps(fs, indent=2) + ';', s, flags=re.S)
open('sw.js', 'w').write(s)
EOF
```

- Keep every path relative (`./`). The site lives under `/hike-planner-3d/`, not at the root of the domain.
- The page only reloads by itself on the hikes list or About, and never twice within 30 seconds, so a host that changes files on every request cannot cause a reload loop.

## Files

- `index.html`: every screen (`home`, `new`, `build`, `view`, `custom`, `about`, `inv`, `detail`), the live guide layer (`#guide`), the header, menu drawer, sheet, dialog, the resume bar (`#resume`) and toast. Scripts are classic scripts loaded in order: theme.js in `<head>`, then core, build, trail, viewer, guide, motion, app.
- `app.css`: all styles. Colours, radii and text size come from CSS variables that js/theme.js sets, so themes and the Customize screen work everywhere. Don't hard-code colours in components. The look (since 1.6) is a dark sports-watch dashboard in the manner of the WHOOP app: black, graphite cards, figures big and labels in small capitals (`.micro`), dials (`.ring`), strips of figures (`.kpis`), and one signal scale: `--good`, `--fair`, `--bad` for how good something is, `--eff` for effort, `--sun` for daylight. Keep text short: a figure or a chart first, words only for labels and alerts.
- `js/theme.js`: themes, accent colours, route palettes, the sky by time of day, and the home-screen skyline drawn from the next hike's real elevation profile. Settings live in localStorage `hp-look`; `lv` marks the settings version: a look saved before 1.5 (no `lv`) moves once from the skyline and rows to the 3D picture and cards, and one before 1.6 (`lv` < 3) to the Carbon theme. Carbon (the default) is always dark: pure black, graphite cards, white main buttons, the red trail mark (`--mark`). Exposes `HP.look`.
- `js/core.js`: the `HP` namespace, IndexedDB `hike-planner-3d` (stores `hikes`, `plans`, `blobs`), reading GPX/KML/GeoJSON, GPX export and helpers in `HP.u`, `HP.sunTimes` (sunrise and sunset in local clock hours, the 3D view's sun model), `HP.effort` (km-effort: km plus 100 m of climbing per point; under 15 easy, 15–25 moderate, 25–35 hard, over 35 very hard) and `HP.compass` (a bearing as N, NNE, …). The inventory (since 1.8): `HP.INVCAT` lists the kinds of kit in groups (clothing, footwear, gear, for the nights, always with me), `HP.INVK` by key, and `HP.inv` keeps the person's items in localStorage `hp-inv` as `{v:1,items:{<kind>:[{name,g}]}}`. The first item of a kind is the one the advice names: `HP.inv.word(kind,fallback)` gives it as `<span class="own">`, or the general word when there is none. Each hike keeps the blobs `<id>:sat` (satellite JPEG), `<id>:topo` (OpenTopoMap JPEG), `<id>:relief` (used for the list picture when there is no satellite picture), `<id>:dem` (the heights at the elevation tiles' full detail: Uint16 in 0.2 m steps above `D.dem.min`, `D.dem.w` × `D.dem.h`, rows from north to south) and the home-screen pictures `<id>:cover` (900 × 1125) and `<id>:coverw` (1600 × 900).
- `js/build.js`: building a plan. `HP.fetchExtras` gives hikes saved before 1.4 their satellite picture and detailed heights the first time they open with a connection. Covers BRouter routing, terrain heights, the topo texture, places on the way (Overpass), and the day split (dynamic programming that prefers huts and villages for nights). It also holds the weather: forecast, ECMWF ensemble, ERA5 climate records, weather types (`HP.WXT`, `HP.wxClass`) and their percentages. Since 1.8 the forecast also brings the wind at 850 and 700 hPa (about 1,500 and 3,000 m: `w8s`, `w8d`, `w7s`, `w7d`, with heights `z8`, `z7`), and the records each day's dominant wind direction and strongest wind (`wd`, `ws`). `HP.climWind(clim,date,type)` gives a weather type's usual wind direction from the days of that type within 30 days of the date, and how steady it is; `HP.fetchClimWind` adds the record winds to hikes saved before 1.8.
- `js/motion.js` (since 2.1): how the app moves. `HP.motion.vt(update,{type,from,to,name})` wraps a DOM change in a view transition (`document.startViewTransition`; Chrome 111+, Safari 18+) and sets `html[data-vt]` so app.css picks the animation: `push` (the new screen slides over from the right, the old one moves a little left and dims, as on iOS), `pop` (the reverse), `expand` (a card grows into its page: the card gets the name `surface`, the page's `.detsurf` and `#detail` get `surface` and `detc`; the shape is the image pair's own background, graphite deepening to black), `collapse` (back into the card), `hero` (a hike's picture grows into the 3D view, both named `pic`) and `fade`. The header is named `top` in every transition, so it stays in place and its title crossfades. Without the API the new screen slides or fades in with the Web Animations API. Presses: a pointerdown on anything pressable shrinks it at once (`scale`, by its size: small buttons 0.95, cards about 0.98) and it springs back on release (a `linear()` spring, damping 0.68); a scroll that starts on it cancels the press. Overlays come in with CSS keyframes on `:not([hidden])` and leave through `HP.motion.close(el,done)`, which plays `.out` and then hides; `HP.motion.open(el)` cancels a close in progress, so use these two instead of setting `hidden` on the drawer, sheet, dialogs, toast, guide and its SOS box. `slider(box)` gives segmented controls and chip rows one indicator (`.segi`) that slides to the chosen option, even across redraws (positions kept by the control's id or first `data-k`). `dragDismiss(box,onClose)`: the hike's menu follows a finger down and closes past a quarter of its height or on a flick. `edgeBack(getPage,goBack)`: a touch from the left edge of a detail page, About, Customize or Inventory drags the page right and goes back past a third of the screen (touch listeners are added only for such a touch). Everything honours `prefers-reduced-motion`, and only transform, opacity and colours animate. The home dials fill once per visit (`drawRings` in app.js); the 3D view shows the hike's own picture until its first frame (`stagePicture`, `.stage.ph`, `.live`), so the picture that grew from the list comes alive; the safety card shimmers while the trail is checked.
- `js/trail.js` (since 2.0): the trail check. `HP.trailCheck(D)` asks Overpass for the OpenStreetMap ways along the route (a union of small boxes, 0.6 km pieces with 60 m around them), the hiking routes they belong to, and the features beside them (cliffs, glaciers, `hazard`, fords, guideposts, rescue points); in Switzerland also the closures and diversions of the Swiss hiking trails (geo.admin.ch identify on `ch.astra.wanderland-sperrungen_umleitungen`), and the countries of the start and end (Nominatim, one request at a time). `analyse` matches each route point to the nearest way within 32 m that runs the same way (a 40 m grid of segments, with a penalty for switching ways and for unmarked roads; gaps of up to 3 points filled, one-point flickers removed), then keeps per point the way's character (`pw` into `paths`: highway, SAC grade, visibility, the waymark `sym` from `osmc:symbol`, marked, informal, access), the waymark runs (`marks`), the hazards (`hz`: `vf`, `aid`, `glacier`, `hazard`, `acc`, `ford`, `inf`, `off` for stretches with no mapped path, `vis`, `closed`, and `cliff` with `top` when the route runs along the edge rather than the foot; a cliff is drawn with its top on the left), the junctions (`junc`: nodes shared by two or more ways, with the turn as S, SL, SR, L, R, HL, HR, KL or KR, `key` when a wrong turn is easy, `town` in villages, and the nearest signpost's name and height) and the stretches on roads. `HP.osmcWords` and `HP.osmcSVG` put a waymark into words (in Switzerland: yellow signs, white-red-white marks, white-blue-white marks) and draw it. The result (`HP.TRAIL_V`) is kept in `D.trail`, so it works offline.
- `js/viewer.js`: the 3D view (three.js r147), walking times, weather simulations, the readout, the day table and the line colours. How the ground is drawn:
  - The ground is a quadtree of Web Mercator tiles (`TILES`), like a web map: tiles split where the camera is close and merge where it is far, with separate tests for the mesh (vertex spacing on screen) and the picture (tile size on screen), some hysteresis, and only for tiles in view. Each tile's heights come from `<id>:dem` (the plan's coarse grid until that is read), with skirts so cracks between levels don't show. Far away every tile shows the picture saved with the hike; close up a tile loads its own picture: for Satellite, SWISSIMAGE aerial pictures from swisstopo up to zoom 18 in and around Switzerland, and ArcGIS World Imagery up to zoom 18 elsewhere and wherever a Swiss picture fails (since 1.9); OpenTopoMap up to zoom 17 for Topo. `prov(t)` gives the pictures to try for a tile, best first, and `t.pf` counts the ones that failed (a 404 just means no picture at that zoom, so the larger tile's shows). Tile pictures are fetched six at a time and the least recently used are dropped. `follow()` keeps the orbit point on the ground and the camera above it when close.
  - Moving around: OrbitControls handles only the mouse (drag turns, right-drag moves, wheel zooms); its `touches` are switched off and touch has its own handling. One finger moves the map so the ground under the finger stays under it, and a flick glides on (its speed comes from the finger's own events, not the frame rate). Two fingers moving together turn the view (sideways) and tilt it (up and down), a twist turns it, and a pinch zooms about the ground between the fingers; each of these starts only past a small threshold, and the fingers are applied once per frame (`touchStep`), both together.
  - The shading detail comes from a texture baked once on the GPU from `<id>:dem` (or from the grid until that is read): red and green hold the slopes for per-pixel light, blue holds how much open sky each point sees (ambient occlusion). It is baked again when the height exaggeration changes. All tiles share one material set up by `WXV.prep`; `uMapRect` says which part of a tile's (or an ancestor's) picture to use.
  - Map styles: `uStyle` 0 = satellite (lightly graded), 1 = topo, 2 = relief, which the shader draws itself from height and slope (treeline and lasting snow from the latitude, contour lines on WebGL 2).
  - One sky colour function (`SKYF`) draws the sky, the haze over distant ground and the soft edges where the model fades into the sky, so they always match. Lambert materials don't get three's `cameraPosition`, hence `uCamP`.
  - The shadow map is fitted to the terrain as the sun sees it, and is redrawn only when the sun moves. The plain map lights the scene from the front left of the first view.
  - Fog sheets, rain and snow fade at the model's edges too. Clouds fade as the camera comes close, turning to light mist rather than a dark smear.
  - Weather (since 1.7, `initWX3D`): two pictures are drawn once on the GPU when the view opens (`bakeTex`). One is the cloud sprites: three cauliflower clusters and a fibrous sheet, holding the surface normal (red, green), how open the surface is (blue, crevices dark) and density. The other is a tileable noise for the cloud and fog layers.
    - Cumulus and storm towers are camera-facing sprites lit per pixel: sun on the side that faces it, blue-grey sky light in the shade, ground light from below, a silver edge against the sun. Each puff carries how much sun reaches it (lower and far-side puffs less), its cloud's flat base (cut in world height, so it stays level from any angle) and how thick its cloud is (storm clouds get dark bases). Anvils are flat sheets in the world, stretched downwind. A cumulus is built from a few large sprites, so it keeps one clear sunlit side.
    - Cloud and fog layers are a lit sea of billows from the noise; peaks rise through with soft, ragged shores (from the height texture `hmT`). Cloud on the slopes (decks, fronts) tints the ground with wisps and a ragged base.
    - Lightning: a jagged channel with forks (`LineSegments2`, additive) and a soft glow of points, in one to four return strokes, only the first lighting the forks; flashes inside the cloud light it from within. Its light on the cloud (`uBolt`) and the ground (`uBoltG`) is stronger in the dark (`flashK`). `V3.strike(true)` makes a strike and holds it, for screenshots.
    - Rain: thin streaks, brightest at the falling end; shafts under storm cells blow downwind at the bottom. Snow: flakes of many sizes that flutter. Below the cloud, falling rain or snow veils the ground with the distance the view travels through it (`uPrK`). Snow on the ground thins on steep slopes and in forest, with rock outcrops and the ground's own light and dark showing through.
  - Wind (since 1.8, `windSetup`, `windBase`, `windAt`): a mass-consistent model in the manner of WindNinja on the plan's coarse grid. The wind above the hills (`windBase`: the forecast's 850/700 hPa winds at ridge height, or for a weather type the direction its days had in the ERA5 records) is made to flow over the terrain by solving ∇·(h∇λ) = −∇·(h v0) with SOR, once each for an east and a north wind and for a stable and a neutral lid, blended by stability. On top: windward speed-up and lee shelter in the manner of MicroMet (exposure from a terrain-position index), channelling along valleys (their axis from the smoothed terrain's curvature), and slope breezes when the wind is light (up by day, down at night). In forecast mode the field is matched to the forecast's 10 m wind at its points (inverse-distance weights on the speed ratio and the turning). Gusts are the forecast's gust margin on top of the local wind. `cond()` uses `windAt` for the readout (its arrow `.warr` turns with the view), the walking times and the wind trails; `WFC` caches it by weather, month, day, hour and forecast issue. `V3.wind(i)` and `V3.windGrid(nx,day,t)` are for tests.
  - Best views (`bestViews`, kept in `D.views` with its version `VIEWS_V`, the route's length and the number of known summits): from points every 200 m, rays over the heights in 48 directions score how far you can see, how high the mountains stand around, how deep the ground drops, whether you stand on a crest, and which named summits are in clear sight. Each day gets its best view if it is a good one, then the best of the rest, at least 2.5 km apart (2 to 6 in all). Each keeps the way with the most to see (`az`) and the skyline's height there (`el`); `viewName` names it after the place it is at or the nearest named place along the trail. The card `#viewscard` and the page `views` list them with buttons to look from there.
  - First person (since 1.8, `fpEnter`, `fpStep`, `fpExit`): the camera walks the route 12 m above the ground at true height (1×, the height setting comes back afterwards), looking 160 m ahead and a little down or up with the slope, at 30 m/s (120 at 4×), with the light and weather of the hour you are there. It stops 5 s at each best view, turned towards it and looking up at the skyline, and 3 s at the end of each day (`fpStops`, which follow changes to the plan). Drag to look around (it eases back while walking), pinch or scroll to zoom; Escape leaves and space pauses. While it runs the canvas's pointer events are taken for looking around (capture listeners), OrbitControls is off, the route lines move down to the ground (`position.y = −13`), the near plane goes to 4 m, and the weather's haze and cloud fog act as for a close view from above (`fogD`). Picking a point on the profile or a day moves the walker; the flyover (`▶ Walk`) and first person don't run together. `V3.look(k)` stops at view k, `V3.firstPerson(km)` walks from km, `V3.fp()` reports the state for tests.
  - Safety (since 2.0, `safety()`, cached by `SAFEK`): runs the trail check when a hike opens with a connection and the check is missing or over 60 days old (`runTrail`; a route from a file gets its grades from it when 60% of it is matched). The grade at each point is the mapped SAC grade, and in Switzerland at least what the waymarks mean (yellow T1, white-red-white T2, white-blue-white T4); tracks and roads count as T1. Findings: T4 and up (bad), T3 (warn), via ferrata, glacier, closure (bad), ladders and chains, hazards, private paths, stretches with no mapped path (bad over 500 m), poor visibility, cliff edges (warn, a note on T1), rock faces above the path, stream crossings (one note), unofficial paths, long unmarked stretches, very steep path (`SLOPE` 22° and up), and the most remote point (half the walking time between huts, villages, lifts, roads and the ends). Steep drops come from the terrain (`exposure()`, needs the detailed heights `HGT`): the gentlest of three 15 m steps from 10 to 55 m out on the lower side, 42° and up where the path is not T1; the terrain alone never makes a route "not for beginners". Edges, drops and steep path inside a T3 or alpine stretch become part of it. The verdict: Suits a beginner, Take care in places, Not for beginners. The card `#safecard` (a strip along the route, the hardest grade, how much is marked, the junctions, the three worst findings, the waymarks to follow, the emergency numbers by country, `SOS`), the page `safety` (the profile coloured by difficulty, the findings day by day with Show on the map, the waymarks in order, the junctions as a turn sheet for each day, help, Open the live guide, Check the trail again), the day's alerts (the two worst findings of the day, made red when the weather there is wet, snowy or stormy) and the line colours `safe`. `V3.exposure()` and `V3.heightAt(x,y)` are for tests; `HP.viewer.safety()` reports the findings, `HP.viewer.show(i)` goes to a point, `HP.viewer.state()` hands the live guide what it needs.
  - Pages behind the cards (since 1.8, `detail(kind)`): each card under the map, and each dial on the home screen, opens a page in the manner of WHOOP: one big figure, a strip of figures, charts drawn at the screen's width (`chart`), the facts in full, and "What it means for you". Kinds: brief (alerts, and the packing list with ticks kept in `D.packed` and the weights of your own items), wx (with the forecast hour by hour), wind (gusts and direction along each day, where it comes from, strong gusts), views, profile (the climbs), daylight, steep and days (with water to carry). `dayFacts`, `kitOf` and `packList` decide what to wear and carry from the day's cold, wet, wind, sun, snow and nights, naming items from the inventory.
  - The route's dark outline and white casing don't write depth and are drawn in order. All three route lines sit 1.5% of their distance closer to the camera, so slopes beside the path don't cut them.
  - Under the map, every fact is shown once: the readout (the point you tap: height big, then km, time, walked, slope, temperature), the day, plan and weather controls, then cards: alerts for each day (`dayBrief`, one short line each, with what to wear and carry), the chance of each weather type with one bar a day and the temperature range on the walk (`renderWxCard`), the profile, daylight (`renderDaylight`: sunrise to sunset, the walk, the time left at arrival, walking after dark in red stripes), steepness (`renderZones`: km in each band, and path grades when known) and the days table (km, up, down, top, walking time coloured over 8 and 10 hours, effort). The long notes live in About.
  - Home-screen pictures: once the satellite picture and the detailed heights are in, `maybeCover` renders the whole route twice into an offscreen target (plain light, no labels), tall and wide, with `fitCover` placing the route clear of the header and of the faded bottom. `D.cover` holds the version (`COVER_V`); change it to draw every hike's pictures again the next time it opens.
  - ArcGIS World Imagery (since 1.9): the owner's ArcGIS Location Platform key is `ESRI_KEY` at the top of js/viewer.js. It allows basemaps only, on the free tier (2 million tiles a month) with pay-as-you-go switched off, so it can never cost anything: past the allowance, or when the key expires (keys last up to a year from November 2026), the service answers with an error instead of a picture. Three such answers in a row (`esriFailed`) switch ArcGIS off for the visit, with one toast, and the other pictures show. Esri's terms allow no offline copies: the service worker never keeps other sites' files, and the home-screen pictures are drawn without ArcGIS tiles (`TILES.cover(true)` in `snap`). Its credit (`#attrib-esri`: Powered by Esri and the data sources, which `esriCredit` reads from the service's own `copyrightText` once a visit and keeps in localStorage `hp-esri-src`) shows whenever its pictures are on screen. To renew the key: location.arcgis.com, your portal, Content > New item > Developer credentials > API key credentials, Public application, no item access, privileges Basemap styles service and Static basemap tiles; paste it into `ESRI_KEY`.
  - The satellite credit (`#attrib`) must stay visible whenever the satellite style is shown: it is a licence condition of Sentinel-2 cloudless, and swisstopo asks for its credit too (`#attrib-ch`, shown while Swiss pictures are on screen). The home screen shows the same credits under its 3D picture and in the footer. On the maps and the home screen the credits are plain text that touches pass through (a finger dragging the map kept opening the sites); on a phone the 3D view's credit is a small line under the map. About keeps the links. Steepness is in degrees, measured over about 200 m of path, in five bands. The walking-time slowdown in bad conditions keeps its own thresholds of about 6°, 11° and 17°.
- `js/guide.js` (since 2.0): the live guide, a layer over the open hike (`openGuide` in app.js pushes a history step `{guide:1}`, so Back closes it; the 3D view is hidden meanwhile, so it rests). A Leaflet map with the hike's own topo picture as an image overlay (works offline; how far it zooms without tiles follows the picture's own detail), and with a connection the Swiss national map (swisstopo, `ch.swisstopo.pixelkarte-farbe`) when the whole route is in Switzerland, else OpenTopoMap. `watchPosition` with high accuracy; each position is matched to the route near the last match (0.8 km back, 3.2 km ahead) and to the whole route when that is far (the first match prefers today's day). Off the route: three positions further than max(40 m, twice the accuracy), never when the accuracy is over 50 m; it sounds (Web Audio; vibration where the phone has it), turns the top red, points the way back (with the compass when it is switched on) and draws a red dashed line. Going back along the route for 100 m shows "Wrong way?". A chime 45 m before each key junction. The top says the next turn and the waymark to follow after it; the panel shows the distance and time to tonight's stop (at your own pace after 25 minutes), arrival, climb left, the next finding of the safety check within 1.5 km, a sunset warning on hike days, the next place. SOS shows the emergency numbers as call links and your position to read out; Share position uses the share sheet. The screen is kept on (Wake Lock, taken again when the app comes back). `localStorage` `hp-guide` remembers a running guide, so the home screen offers to continue it after the phone closed the app (`offerResume`). `HP.guide.fix(lat,lon,acc,t)` and `HP.guide.state()` are for tests.
- Find an easier way (since 2.1, `HP.easier` in build.js, `findEasier` in app.js): the safety check's findings that a way round can avoid (`sac`, `vf`, `glacier`, `closed`, `aid`, `drop`, `edge`, `off`, `vis`, `hazard`, not just notes) are grouped when under 2 km apart. For each group BRouter is asked for a way between points 1.5 km and then 5 km before and after it (only 1.5 km for drops and cliff edges on ordinary paths), with no-go circles (40 m) along the hard place and `SAC_scale_limit` 2; a group with a must-avoid place (an alpine stretch, a via ferrata, a glacier or a closure) is tried with 3 after that. A way round is kept when its detour is at most 80% of the stretch it replaces, at least 3 km and at most 15 km (for drops and edges half of it, at least 1.5 km). The rest of the route stays as it was. The dialog compares distance, climbing and the hardest grade, lists what it goes round and what is left, and Create this hike builds it as a new hike named "· easier" through the shared build flow (`runBuild(input,opts,from)`: Cancel goes back to the hike it came from, which stays as it was; the new one opens on top of it, so Back compares them). `HP.route(points,signal,{sac,nogos})` takes the grade limit and no-go circles.
- `js/app.js`: hash routes (`#/`, `#/new`, `#/hike/<id>`, `#/customize`, `#/inventory`, `#/about`), the home screen, menu, Customize, the Inventory (`renderInv`, `editItem`: each kind with your items as chips, + to add, a dialog to rename, weigh, delete or make an item the first choice), the planning map (Leaflet), the build flow and update handling. A card's page opens under the hike's own address with a `history.pushState({det})` step (`openDetail`, `syncDetail`), so Back closes it; a dial on the home screen opens the next hike with its page (`pendingDet`), and "Look from here" on the views page returns to the map in first person (`pendingLook`). The home screen's default background is the next hike's 3D picture (tall on screens under 900 px, wide above), fading into a blurred copy of itself. Under the title, three dials: the chance of fair weather (from the forecast runs or the records), the effort of the hardest day, and the daylight left on the tightest day (`dayFigures` in the hike summary, from the plan's walking times at the chosen start, pace and rests); then distance, climbing and the highest point, and the forecast for each day; a next hike without a picture yet shows the skyline, and with no hike ahead it shows the latest hike's picture or the sample. The list shows each hike's wide picture as a card. `COV` keeps one object URL per picture.
- `sw.js`: the offline copy and the update check.
- `lib/` holds three.js r147 (with OrbitControls and Line2) and Leaflet 1.9.4. `fonts/` holds Barlow and Barlow Semi Condensed. `icons/` holds the app icons, `img/contours.svg` is a background, and `img/sample-tall.jpg` and `img/sample-wide.jpg` are the sample pictures (the Bärentrek, rendered by the app with `V3.cover(w,h)`) for a home screen with no hike.

## Data sources

All are free. All but one need no key: BRouter (`hiking-mountain` profile), Overpass and Nominatim (also for the trail check), the Swiss hiking trail closures (geo.admin.ch, open government data), the Swiss national map for the live guide (swisstopo, free with the credit), OpenTopoMap tiles, AWS Terrain Tiles (terrarium), Sentinel-2 cloudless 2025 by EOX (`tiles.maps.eox.at`, zoom ≤ 13, free for personal use with the credit shown), SWISSIMAGE by swisstopo (`wmts.geo.admin.ch`, open government data, free with the source credited; it covers Switzerland and some of the land around it), Open-Meteo for the forecast, the ensemble (`ecmwf_ifs025`) and the archive (ERA5), and ArcGIS World Imagery by Esri (`ibasemaps-api.arcgis.com`, close-ups outside Switzerland, with the owner's free-tier key; see above).

## Testing

The tests are Playwright scripts in `tests/`, run in headless Chrome. They use `/usr/bin/google-chrome` if it exists; otherwise set `CHROME` or let Playwright use its own Chromium. Weather and Overpass calls are mocked in `tests/mocks.py`, because the archive API rate-limits and Overpass times out from sandboxes. ArcGIS pictures are a made-up picture (`tests/esri_mock.jpg`) so tests don't use the key's allowance; `ESRI=live` lets them through. `V3.orbitAt(i,dist,az,pol)` points the view at route point i, for screenshots. Run them from the repository root:

```sh
pip install playwright --break-system-packages   # if missing
python3 -m http.server 8777 &                    # serves the repository
python3 tests/redirect_server.py &               # port 8778, a host that redirects index.html
BASE=http://localhost:8777/ python3 tests/test_app.py desktop    # also: mobile, offline
BASE=http://localhost:8778/ python3 tests/test_app.py offline
python3 tests/test_misc.py
python3 tests/test_home.py full                  # also: empty
python3 tests/test_home2.py
python3 tests/test_wx_prob.py
python3 tests/test_update.py                     # serves its own copy on 8791; OLD=<folder or zip> also tests a switch from an older version
BASE=http://localhost:8777/ python3 tests/test_extras.py   # a hike from before 1.4 gets its satellite picture and heights
python3 tests/test_touch.py                      # one finger moves the map, two fingers turn, tilt, twist and pinch
BASE=http://localhost:8777/ python3 tests/test_v18.py   # inventory, the pages behind the cards, the wind, best views and first person
BASE=http://localhost:8777/ python3 tests/test_guide.py  # the trail check, safety and the live guide with a simulated GPS
BASE=http://localhost:8777/ python3 tests/test_v21.py    # the easier way, page transitions, presses, sliding indicators, overlays, reduced motion
```

BRouter is mocked with a path that bows out to one side (`mocks.brouter`); `BROUTER=live` lets the real router answer. In the test browser the 3D view draws slowly (software WebGL), so check motion on screens without it, or pause the transition's animations (`document.getAnimations()`) and draw chosen moments, as the motion checks did. The trail check's Overpass query is answered from real OpenStreetMap data around the Bärentrek (`tests/osm_baerentrek.json.gz`, ODbL); geo.admin.ch and Nominatim's country lookup are mocked too. `tests/test_guide.py` feeds positions with `HP.guide.fix` and once through the browser's own geolocation.

Screenshots go to `tests/shots/`, which git ignores. Look at them after any visual change: the 3D view and the home screen break easily in ways the scripts don't catch.

## Conventions

- Interface text is plain and in sentence case, with no jargon. The person using it is a beginner hiker.
- Units are metric, the minus sign is `−`, and steepness is shown in degrees.
- The 3D view should look premium: real light and shade, haze with distance, no hard edges. Check changes there on phone-sized and desktop screenshots.
- Keep the app working offline for saved hikes, and keep it fast on a phone.
- Commit messages say what changed for the person using the app.
